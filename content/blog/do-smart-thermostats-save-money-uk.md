Heating is the single biggest line on most UK energy bills - typically more than half of everything a household spends on gas and electricity across the year. So it is a fair question to ask before you spend £100 to £220 on new kit: do smart thermostats actually save money, or are they just a nicer app on the wall?

The honest answer is that a smart thermostat saves money for most households, but the size of the saving depends far more on how you use it than on which brand you buy. This guide explains exactly how the savings happen, how much you can realistically expect off your heating bill in 2026, which smart thermostats are worth buying in the UK, and who benefits most.

## The short answer: do smart thermostats save money?

Yes, for most homes. A smart thermostat typically trims somewhere between 8% and 16% off your heating bill once it is set up properly and you actually use its features. On a typical UK gas heating bill of around £850 a year, that is roughly £70 to £135 saved annually. Because a good smart thermostat costs £100 to £220 fitted, most households pay it back within one to three heating seasons and then keep the saving every year afterwards.

The saving is not automatic, though. Bolt a smart thermostat onto a heating system and never touch the schedule and you will save very little. The gains come from the behaviour changes the device makes easy: heating only the hours you are home, turning down when you leave, and never heating an empty house because you forgot to press a button. If you already run a tight manual schedule and never overheat, your saving will sit at the lower end. If your current setup is "boiler on all day" or a mechanical dial nobody understands, the saving can be much larger.

## How a smart thermostat saves you money

There are four mechanisms doing the work, and understanding them helps you get the most out of the device.

**Precise scheduling.** Old mechanical and even basic digital programmers give you one or two crude on/off windows. A smart thermostat lets you set different temperatures for different times of day and different days of the week, down to the half hour. Heating the house to 18C while you sleep instead of 21C, and only reaching full temperature 30 minutes before you get up, quietly removes hours of wasted burn every week.

**Geofencing and presence detection.** This is the feature that separates smart thermostats from a good timer. The thermostat uses your phone's location to know when the house is empty and turns the heating down, then brings it back up as you head home. Nobody remembers to turn the heating off before an unexpected trip out - your phone does it for you.

**Learning and weather compensation.** Better models learn how long your home takes to warm up and adjust the start time so you are not heating early "just in case". Some also pull the local forecast so the boiler works less hard on a mild day. These small optimisations add up across a winter.

**Visibility.** Simply seeing your heating usage in an app changes behaviour. It is the same effect a smart meter in-home display has - once you can see the cost of nudging the thermostat up two degrees, you do it less often. ## How much can you realistically save?

The Energy Saving Trust has long estimated that good heating controls and thermostatic radiator valves together can save a meaningful chunk of a heating bill. A smart thermostat is the modern, easier-to-use version of those controls. Here is a realistic range based on how much room your current setup leaves for improvement.

| Your current setup | Realistic annual saving | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical dial, heating on all day | £110 - £180 | Most to gain - you are removing large chunks of wasted heating |
| Basic timer, one or two on/off windows | £70 - £120 | Tighter scheduling plus geofencing close the gaps |
| Tight manual schedule, already careful | £30 - £60 | Mostly from presence detection and never forgetting |
| Heat pump with weather compensation | £40 - £90 | Savings come from zoning and avoiding short-cycling |

These figures assume a typical three-bedroom UK home on gas. Larger homes and homes that were previously badly controlled sit higher; small, already-efficient flats sit lower. Your own heating bill is the anchor - if heating is £700 a year, do not expect to save £180.

## Smart thermostats and heat pumps

If you have a heat pump, or you are weighing one up, the thermostat question changes slightly. Heat pumps run most efficiently at a low, steady flow temperature rather than the hot bursts a gas boiler gives. That means the biggest heat pump savings come from weather compensation and from avoiding the short-cycling that happens when a thermostat keeps switching the system fully on and off.

Not every smart thermostat is heat-pump friendly, so check compatibility before you buy - some are designed only for on/off boiler control. ## What about smart radiator valves?

Smart thermostatic radiator valves, or smart TRVs, are the natural companion to a smart thermostat. A single thermostat controls the whole heating system from one point - usually the hallway. Smart TRVs let each room have its own schedule and target temperature, so you can keep a home office warm during the day while the bedrooms stay cool until the evening.

For homes where different rooms are used at very different times, [smart radiator valves](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=smart+radiator+thermostat+valve+trv&tag=thehomeenergyhub-21) can add more saving than the central thermostat on its own. The trade-off is cost - you need one per radiator you want to control, and they add up. Most people start with the thermostat, live with it for a season, and add valves to the two or three rooms where zoning clearly helps.

## Will a smart thermostat save you money?

To bring it together, here is who benefits most and least.

You will save the most if your current heating is poorly controlled - a mechanical dial, no schedule, or a house that is often heated while empty. You will also gain a lot if your household routine is irregular, because geofencing removes the guesswork.

You will save less if you already run a disciplined manual schedule, never overheat, and are reliably home at the same times. In that case the saving is real but modest, and the main benefit is convenience rather than a dramatic bill cut.

Either way, a smart thermostat only pays back if the rest of your home is holding onto the heat it makes. There is no point precisely scheduling heat that leaks straight out through an uninsulated loft or draughty doors. ## How to get the most savings from your smart thermostat

Buying the device is step one. These habits are where the money actually comes from:

- Set a realistic schedule that matches when you are genuinely home, not an optimistic one you will override every evening.
- Turn on geofencing or presence detection and let it manage the "we went out" gaps.
- Drop your daytime and overnight target by one or two degrees - each degree is worth roughly 6% to 10% off heating.
- Use zoning or smart TRVs to stop heating rooms nobody is in.
- Check the app once a week for the first month so you can tune the schedule to real life.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a smart thermostat cost to run?

Almost nothing. The thermostat itself draws a tiny amount of standby power, on the order of a pound or two a year. The whole point of the device is that it saves far more on heating than it uses in electricity.

### Do smart thermostats work with any boiler?

Most work with standard UK combi and system boilers running on gas or oil. Compatibility issues are more common with heat pumps, some older heating systems, and homes with unusual wiring. Always check the manufacturer's compatibility checker before buying, and use a heating engineer if you are not confident with the wiring.

### Can I fit a smart thermostat myself?

If you are comfortable and competent with mains wiring, many are designed for DIY fitting and come with clear guides. If not, a heating engineer will typically fit one for £60 to £120. Getting the wiring right matters, so do not force it if you are unsure.

### Do smart thermostats need a subscription?

The core heating and scheduling features on Hive, Nest, tado and Drayton Wiser do not need a subscription. Some brands offer optional paid tiers for extra automation, but you can save money on your heating without paying anything on top of the hardware.

### Will a smart thermostat lower my bills in summer?

The savings are almost entirely about heating, so the benefit shows up in the colder months. In summer, when the heating is off, there is little to save - which is exactly why late summer and early autumn are the smart time to fit one, ready for the season when it earns its keep.

## The bottom line

Smart thermostats save money for the large majority of UK households, usually £70 to £135 a year off a typical gas heating bill, and they pay for themselves within one to three winters. The device matters less than how you use it: schedule honestly, use presence detection, nudge the target down a degree or two, and keep the heat in with decent insulation. Do that, and a smart thermostat is one of the more reliable energy-saving upgrades you can make in 2026.
