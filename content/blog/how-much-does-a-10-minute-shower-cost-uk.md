So, how much does a 10-minute shower cost in the UK? At the current Ofgem price cap, a 10-minute shower costs about **42p in electricity on a 9.5kW electric shower**, or about **31p in gas on a combi boiler**. If you have a water meter, add roughly **19p of water for the electric shower and 38p for the combi**, because the combi shower uses about twice as much water.

Put the two together and the answer most people find surprising is that, for a metered household, a 10-minute electric shower (about **61p**) and a 10-minute combi shower (about **69p**) cost almost the same. Electricity is three times the price of gas per kWh, but an electric shower uses half the water, and water is not cheap either.

This guide shows the cost per minute for every common shower type, how 5, 10 and 15 minute showers compare, whether electric showers are expensive to run, and the changes that actually bring the cost down.

## The rates these figures use

Every figure below uses the Ofgem price cap for 1 October to 31 December 2026: **26.32p per kWh for electricity** and **7.97p per kWh for gas**. There is no VAT on electricity from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. These are the same rates we use across all our [appliance running cost guides](/running-costs).

For water we use **0.42p per litre**, which is Thames Water's 2026/27 metered charge for clean water plus wastewater (£2.73 plus £1.47 per cubic metre). Other water companies charge a little more or less, but most metered customers pay somewhere between 0.3p and 0.6p per litre once sewerage is included.

We also assume a winter shower: cold water coming in at about 10°C and a shower at about 40°C. That 30°C rise is what the energy pays for, and it is the single biggest driver of the cost.

If you are **not on a water meter**, your water bill is fixed and the extra water costs you nothing at the margin, so only the energy column applies. In that case the combi is clearly the cheaper shower and the electric shower costs about a third more.

## How the cost of an electric shower is worked out

An electric shower is the easiest appliance in the house to cost, because it runs at a fixed power the whole time it is on. The sum is:

**Power (kW) x minutes / 60 x your unit rate = cost**

A 9.5kW shower running for 10 minutes uses 9.5 x 10 / 60 = **1.58 kWh**. At 26.32p per kWh that is **41.7p**.

The number you need is printed on the shower unit or in the manual, and it is usually one of 7.5kW, 8.5kW, 9.5kW, 10.5kW or 10.8kW. Most homes fitted in the last decade have a 9.5kW model.

### Electric shower running cost per minute

| Shower rating | Cost per minute | 5 minutes | 10 minutes | 15 minutes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.5kW | 3.3p | 16p | 33p | 49p |
| 8.5kW | 3.7p | 19p | 37p | 56p |
| 9.5kW | 4.2p | 21p | 42p | 63p |
| 10.5kW | 4.6p | 23p | 46p | 69p |

One thing these tables make clear is that **a more powerful electric shower costs more per minute but does not waste energy**. A 10.5kW shower gives you a stronger, hotter flow, and the extra cost buys you extra hot water. Every electric shower turns all its electricity into heat in the water. The only way to spend less is to use less hot water: shorter showers, or a lower power setting.

Most electric showers have two or three power settings. The "eco" or low setting typically runs at around half power, which halves the cost per minute and is fine in summer when the water coming in is warmer.

## How the cost of a combi or mixer shower is worked out

A mixer shower fed by a combi boiler takes a little more arithmetic, because the cost depends on how much water comes out of the head.

1. **Litres used** = flow rate x minutes. A typical mixer shower runs at about 9 litres a minute, so 10 minutes is 90 litres.
2. **Heat needed** = litres x 0.035 kWh for a 30°C temperature rise. For 90 litres that is 3.14 kWh.
3. **Gas used** = heat needed / boiler efficiency. Combis are less efficient on hot water than on heating, so we use 80%. That is 3.92 kWh of gas.
4. **Cost** = 3.92 x 7.97p = **31.3p**.

The flow rate matters far more than most people realise. Swap a 9 l/min head for a 7 l/min eco head and the same 10-minute shower falls from 31p of gas to 24p, and from 90 litres of water to 70. On a high-pressure system with a rainfall head running at 12 to 15 litres a minute, it can climb past 45p of gas and 50p of water.

You can measure your own flow rate in a minute: hold a bucket marked in litres under the shower for 10 seconds and multiply what you collect by six.

## Are electric showers expensive to run?

Per minute, yes, a little: a 9.5kW electric shower costs about 4.2p a minute in electricity against roughly 3.1p a minute in gas for a combi mixer shower. Per unit of heat, electricity is around three times the price of gas, so if you only compare energy bills, the combi always wins.

But that is not the whole bill. An electric shower heats the water as it passes through, so its flow is limited by how much heat it can add. In winter a 9.5kW unit delivers about 4.5 litres a minute, roughly half the flow of a typical combi mixer shower. That means:

- **On a water meter**, the lower water use largely cancels out the higher energy price. A 10-minute 9.5kW electric shower costs about 61p in total against about 69p for a 9 l/min combi shower.
- **Without a water meter**, the extra water is free at the margin, so the electric shower costs about 42p against 31p for the combi.
- **Against an immersion-heated cylinder on a standard tariff**, an electric shower is much cheaper. It only heats the water you actually use, while a cylinder heated by an immersion loses heat all day and pays the full electricity rate to make it. Our [immersion heater running costs guide](/blog/immersion-heater-running-costs-uk) covers that comparison in more detail.

So electric showers are not expensive to run in the way people often fear. They are a cheap way to shower if you are metered, a cheap backup if your boiler fails, and far cheaper than using an immersion to heat a whole cylinder for one person's shower. Where they lose is against a gas combi in an unmetered home.

## What about a heat pump?

A heat pump heating a hot water cylinder typically runs at a coefficient of performance of about 2.5 for hot water, meaning it puts out about 2.5 kWh of heat for every kWh of electricity. After allowing for heat lost from the cylinder, a 10-minute, 9 l/min shower costs about **37p** in electricity at the standard cap rate. That is a little more than a combi's 31p of gas, and it narrows further if you are on a heat-pump tariff with a cheaper overnight rate for heating the cylinder.

## How much does a shower cost per year?

Daily 10-minute showers add up. Here is what one person showering once a day costs over a year (365 showers), energy only:

| Shower type | Cost per shower (energy) | Per year, one person | Per year, two people |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electric shower, 8.5kW | 37p | £136 | £272 |
| Electric shower, 9.5kW | 42p | £152 | £304 |
| Combi boiler, 7 l/min eco head | 24p | £89 | £177 |
| Combi boiler, 9 l/min mixer | 31p | £114 | £228 |
| Immersion cylinder, standard rate | 92p | £335 | £670 |

Add the water if you are metered: 45 litres a day is about £70 a year per person, and 90 litres is about £138. Showers are one of the larger lines on a metered water bill, and every minute you cut saves on both bills at once.

These are winter figures. In summer the water from the mains is warmer, often around 15 to 18°C, so a combi or cylinder needs roughly 20% to 25% less energy for the same shower, and an electric shower can be switched to its lower setting. A year-round average is a little lower than the table above.

## 5, 10 and 15 minute showers compared

| Length | 9.5kW electric (energy) | 9.5kW electric (with water) | Combi 9 l/min (energy) | Combi 9 l/min (with water) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 minutes | 21p | 30p | 16p | 35p |
| 10 minutes | 42p | 61p | 31p | 69p |

Cutting a 10-minute shower to 5 minutes saves about 30p to 35p a time on a metered supply. For two people showering daily, that is about **£220 to £250 a year**, which is more than most energy-saving gadgets will ever return.

## Is a shower cheaper than a bath?

Usually, yes, as long as the shower is short. A typical bath holds about 80 litres of hot water at 40°C. Heated by a combi, that is about 28p of gas plus 34p of water on a meter, about **61p** in total. That is about the same as a 9-minute combi shower at 9 l/min, or a 10-minute electric shower. A 5-minute shower is cheaper than a bath on any system. A 15-minute power shower costs more than a bath.

## How to cut the cost of your shower

The good news is that most of the savings here cost little or nothing.

1. **Shorten it.** This is the biggest lever by far. Every minute off a combi shower saves about 7p on a meter, or 3p in gas alone. A simple [shower timer](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=shower+timer&tag=thehomeenergyhub-21) helps more than you might expect, particularly with teenagers.

2. **Fit a water-saving head on a mixer shower.** An [aerating or low-flow shower head](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=water+saving+shower+head&tag=thehomeenergyhub-21) cuts flow from 9 or more litres a minute to 6 or 7 while keeping the feel of the spray. On a combi with a water meter, going from 9 to 7 l/min saves about 15p per 10-minute shower, so a £15 to £25 head pays for itself in a couple of months. Many water companies give them away free, so check your supplier's website first. Do not fit one to an electric shower: an electric shower's flow is already low, and restricting it can trip the unit's safety cut-out.

3. **Use the eco setting on an electric shower when it is warm enough.** Half power means half the cost per minute, and from late spring to early autumn it is usually hot enough.

4. **Turn down the boiler's hot water temperature.** On a combi, setting the hot water to 50 to 55°C means you mix in less cold water and the boiler runs more efficiently. Our guide to [boiler temperature settings](/blog/what-temperature-should-my-boiler-be-set-at) explains which dial to change and what to leave alone.

5. **Do not heat a whole cylinder with the immersion for one shower.** If your boiler is out of action and you have both an electric shower and an immersion, the electric shower is far cheaper. If you only have an immersion, use a boost timer rather than leaving it on.

6. **Descale the shower head.** In hard-water areas limescale blocks the spray holes, which pushes you to stand under the shower for longer to rinse properly. Soaking the head in white vinegar every few months keeps it clear.

7. **Think about a water meter if you live alone or as a couple.** Smaller households in larger homes often pay less on a meter than on rateable value. Your water company can tell you whether you are likely to save, and most let you switch back within a set period if you do not. Once you are metered, shorter showers and an eco head cut both bills at once.

8. **Watch the cost happen.** A smart meter's in-home display shows an electric shower's draw jump to 9kW or more the moment it is switched on, which tends to make everyone in the house aware of it very quickly. If yours has stopped working, see our guide to the [best in-home displays and energy monitors](/blog/best-smart-meter-in-home-display-uk).

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a 10-minute shower cost in the UK?

About 42p in electricity on a 9.5kW electric shower, or about 31p in gas on a combi boiler with a typical 9 l/min mixer shower, at the October to December 2026 price cap. If you are on a water meter, add about 19p of water for the electric shower and about 38p for the combi, making totals of around 61p and 69p.

### How much does it cost to run an electric shower per minute?

At 26.32p per kWh, a 7.5kW electric shower costs 3.3p a minute, an 8.5kW shower 3.7p, a 9.5kW shower 4.2p and a 10.5kW shower 4.6p. Multiply the shower's kW rating by your unit rate and divide by 60 for your own figure.

### Are electric showers expensive to run?

Not especially. Per minute they cost a little more in energy than a gas combi shower, about 4.2p against 3.1p, because electricity costs around three times as much as gas per kWh. But they use roughly half as much water, so on a water meter a 10-minute electric shower costs about the same as, or slightly less than, a combi shower. They are far cheaper than heating a whole cylinder with an immersion heater.

### How much does a 5-minute shower cost?

About 21p in electricity on a 9.5kW electric shower, or about 16p in gas on a combi. With metered water the totals are about 30p and 35p.

### Is it cheaper to shower with an electric shower or the boiler?

If you are not on a water meter, the gas boiler is cheaper: about 31p against 42p for 10 minutes. If you are on a water meter, the electric shower is usually slightly cheaper overall because it uses about half the water. If your hot water comes from an immersion heater on a standard tariff, the electric shower is much cheaper.

### How much water does a 10-minute shower use?

About 45 litres on a 9.5kW electric shower in winter, about 90 litres on a typical combi mixer shower, and 120 to 150 litres on a power shower or high-flow rainfall head.

## The bottom line

A 10-minute shower costs about **42p in electricity on a 9.5kW electric shower** or **31p in gas on a combi boiler**. If you have a water meter, the electric shower's lower water use closes most of that gap, and the two cost around **60p to 70p** in total. The expensive shower is not the electric one: it is a long, high-flow power shower, or a whole cylinder heated by an immersion on a standard tariff.

The cheapest shower is a shorter one. Cutting from 10 minutes to 5 saves about a third of a pound a time on a metered supply, and an eco head on a mixer shower cuts the cost of every minute that remains.
